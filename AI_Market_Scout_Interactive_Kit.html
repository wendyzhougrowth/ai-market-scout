Start selling solutions.

2-minute AI perception test

What does AI say about your shop?

Ask an AI tool: “What does [Your Company] do?” Then try a supplier-category query. A wrong description suggests a FIX. No meaningful visibility suggests a FILL.

The principle

Lead with the solution. Back it with specifications.

Certifications and equipment are credible proof points. Connect them to the buyer’s actual technical or business requirements—without claiming work you haven't done.

A question

Who actually owns this problem?
Ask AI to identify three likely decision-maker roles for manufacturing subcontracts. Confirm actual titles and identities from public sources.
What keeps them up at night?
Explore likely risk priorities: milestones, material traceability, supply continuity, production ramp, and lead times. Treat these as hypotheses until verified.
The 3-sentence outreach formula
SENTENCE 1
We noticed [trigger / regulatory shift / project] is creating a need for [capability].
SENTENCE 2
We're a [certification]-certified shop already solving this for [existing industry].
SENTENCE 3
Would it be useful to compare notes on how you're approaching [specific need]?
Wendy's original AI drafting prompt
Write a 3-sentence email to a [VP of Engineering] at [Company]. Mention their recent [trigger]. Pivot from our [AS9100 aerospace] background to their specific need for [capability]. Follow this structure: noticed the trigger, who we are and what we already solve, and a closing question. Keep it technical, direct and free of business fluff.
Before · Generic vendor message
“Hi, I wanted to reach out about our machining capabilities. We offer competitive pricing and fast turnaround. Let me know if you'd like a quote.”
Problem: Nothing here explains why the buyer should engage now.
After · Triggered outreach (illustrative template)
“We noticed [Company] is scaling production for [project] following your recent [verified announcement]. We're a [City]-based, [certification]-certified shop with [relevant verified capability]. Would it be useful to compare notes on [specific technical requirement]?”
Why it's stronger: Timely, relevant, and focused on the customer's need.

One-page AI policy
Protect the shop while using AI.
AI is useful for public-data research and drafting. Confidential shop or customer information needs a different level of protection.
✓ Safe to do with public information
  • Use public machine specs and certifications to improve website descriptions.
  • Research public trigger sources such as port bids, WSDOT notices, and JBLM announcements.
  • Draft general outreach from the three-sentence template without exposing confidential customer information.
✕ Never put into public AI tools
  • Customer lists, confidential pricing, or proprietary drawings.
  • RFQ details, IP, or restricted engineering documents.
  • Anything a customer has requested remain confidential.
The rule: If it's proprietary, keep it offline. For sensitive work, use an appropriately approved enterprise/private AI environment and check with your IT or security team. An enterprise subscription alone does not override contractual handling restrictions.
